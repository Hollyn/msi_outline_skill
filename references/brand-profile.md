# Brand profile

One file per client, stored at `clients/<client-slug>.md`. Written once, corrected
as you learn things, read at the start of every brief for that client.

Its job is narrow: supply the parts of a brief that cannot be read off a SERP.
The SERP tells you what is ranking. This file tells you what this particular
client can say about it that nobody else on that SERP can.

## Template

```markdown
# <Client name>

**Site:** <domain>
**Country:** <two-letter code for Ahrefs and for reading the SERP>
**What they sell:** <the actual commercial offer, in one line>
**Default CTA:** <where posts should land — booking, trial, consultation>

## Who they serve

<The ideal customer, specifically. Not "parents" — "parents of 3–6 year olds
considering leaving traditional school mid-year." The narrower this is, the
better the angles that come out of it.>

## What they know that competitors don't

<The heart of the file. Lived experience, proprietary data, a contrarian
position they can defend, a practitioner's view the generalist publishers
ranking for these keywords do not have. Two or three specifics beat a
paragraph of positioning language.>

## Positions and constraints

<Claims they will not make, competitors they will not name, regulatory or
professional limits on what can be said, topics they consider settled.>

## Voice

<Only what changes an outline: reading level, whether first-person is allowed,
whether they use humour. Not a full style guide — the writers have that.>

## Internal links

<Cornerstone pages new posts should point back to.>
```

## What makes a field usable

The test for every line is whether it would change an outline. "We're passionate
about quality education" would not. "The founder taught in both systems for
eleven years and thinks the transition year is where most families fail" would —
it produces a section no competitor can write.

Write specifics, not positioning. If a sentence could appear on a competitor's
about page, it does not belong here.

## Keeping it current

Update the file when a brief teaches you something durable about the client, and
say that you did. A founder's aside during review, a claim the editor struck, a
CTA that changed — each is worth more than the brief it came from, because every
future brief inherits it.

Do not record anything you have not confirmed with the client or their team.
An invented differentiator in this file becomes an invented differentiator in
every brief that follows it.
