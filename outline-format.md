# Outline format

The outline is a roadmap for the writer: which topics, in which order, with what nested underneath, and what each section needs to accomplish.

## Instructional bullets

Bullets tell the writer what to cover and why. They do not write the sentence for them.

**Wrong** (prose — puts words in the writer's mouth):
> The reason the teacher's role matters is that it fundamentally shapes how a child experiences learning day to day.

**Right** (instructional — sets intent, leaves execution open):
> - Introduce the teacher's role as one of the most significant differences between the two models.
> - Expand on the traditional teacher: delivers instruction, manages pacing, assesses against standardised benchmarks.
> - Expand on the alternative: observes, prepares the environment, introduces material at the right developmental moment, then steps back.
> - Reinforce that neither is passive — they serve different functions depending on the child's needs.

Note what the right version does: it establishes *why the section exists*, gives the writer the beats in order, and states the position the section should land on — without supplying a single publishable sentence.

## Depth calibration

Roughly one H2 per 250–350 words of target length, with H3s nested underneath where a topic genuinely splits. A 1,500-word post supports about four or five H2s with sub-points. Going far beyond that produces a brief that looks thorough and reads thin.

## Worked example (abbreviated)

```
# Content Brief: montessori vs traditional education

**Target keyword:** montessori vs traditional education
**Word count:** 1,800
**Search intent:** Blog post / Comparison / Parent choosing a school

## Recommended outline

### H2: Introduction
- Introduce the comparison many parents face when choosing an
  educational approach.
- Acknowledge that the differences go deeper than classroom setup or
  teaching style.
- Reassure the reader this isn't about declaring one approach superior —
  it's about making an informed decision.
- Preview what the article covers: how each approach works, the key
  differences, why they matter, how to find the right fit.

### H2: The classroom: what each environment looks like

#### H3: The traditional classroom
- Describe the physical setup and how it reflects the teaching model.

#### H3: The Montessori classroom
- Contrast the setup; connect the environment to child-led learning.

### H2: The role of the teacher: guide vs instructor
- [instructional bullets as above]

### H2: Take the next step
- CTA: book a free consultation.
- Keep it a natural continuation of the decision framework, not a pitch.
```

## What earns a place in the brief

Every section should answer: what does the reader get here, and why does it sit at this point in the sequence? If neither has an answer, cut it.
